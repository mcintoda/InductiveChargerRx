Curb drain clip, in section

Every cross-section of the clip that drains the puddle off a flat granite shower curb. All drawings are to scale from the same model that was simulated and that you upload to JLC. Water runs down the fixed glass panel, collects on the curb against the silicone bead, enters the clip at the comb, runs along 13 capillary grooves, over the edge and down to a drip tip above the tile.

Side section through the curb

Nothing is exaggerated: at 2.6 mm the clip is barely thicker than the puddle it sits in. Red dash-dot lines mark where sections A and C are cut.

AGrooved arm, cut across the curb

The grooves are V-shaped so their corners pull water along by capillary action. Their floors sit 1.3 mm above the stone, below the 1.6 mm depth the puddle thins to, so the water stays connected to them.

BOne groove, and how water sits in it

Drawn 150× actual size. Gravity on the leg pulls the water, and the meniscus in each groove responds by curving. The more it curves, the harder it pulls, until it recedes into the bottom of the V. The flow model was solved for each of these shapes.

The rule that decides it: water creeps along the groove by itself only if its contact angle θ with the nylon is below 90° − α = 69°. Fresh nylon can be above that. A wash with dish soap brings it well below.

CComb inlet by the silicone bead

For the last 8 mm of the arm, each groove becomes a slot cut right through to the granite. Water enters at stone level, so the clip keeps draining after the puddle is thinner than the grooves are high.

DDrip tip, seen from inside the shower

The leg's grooves carry the water down. The V point at the bottom collects it into one place so drops fall cleanly onto the tile instead of creeping back under the clip.

Why the arm has to sit on the stone

Heights in mm. The puddle thins to about 1.6 mm before it starts to shrink. A millimetre of tape sounds harmless, but it lifts every groove out of reach. Tape the vertical leg to the curb's inner face and let the arm lie directly on the granite.

A′Cloth version, same cut

The alternative print holds a microfiber strip in one wide groove. It drains about 30 times more slowly than wetted grooves, but it pulls the puddle down to a damp film.

Method: Stokes flow solved on each groove cross-section (checked against exact pipe solutions to 0.3%), coupled to a capillary and gravity flow model along the arm, bend and leg, and a draining-puddle model. Water at 30 °C. Contact angles on the nylon and granite were not measured, so the results cover a range. Fresh, unwashed nylon may bead water; wash the clip with dish soap before use.
